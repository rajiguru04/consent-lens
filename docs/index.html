Can I take back a consent I gave? Do they delete what they already collected?

answered with citation

The consent-expiry vs data-retention distinction — or honest abstention if the deletion rule lives outside this corpus.

Yes, you can revoke consent at any time, and the Account Aggregator must provide you a way to do so. The regulations say data can only be used "as may be specified in the consent artefact," but they do not expressly require the recipient (Financial Information User) to delete data already collected before you revoked. What happens to already-collected data is not settled by the RBI Master Direction.
— RBI Master Direction — NBFC Account Aggregator (Reserve Bank) Directions, 2016, cl. 6.6 (retrieved 2026-08-20)
— RBI Master Direction — NBFC Account Aggregator (Reserve Bank) Directions, 2016, cl. 7.6.2 (retrieved 2026-08-20)

Stop and act on this now, before reading anything else.

What you've described involves a one-time passcode being shared — that is different from sharing information, because it can authorise a transaction.

1. Call your bank's fraud helpline immediately. Use the number printed on your card or in your bank's own app — not a number from a message or from whoever contacted you.
2. Block the card or freeze the account through your bank's app if you can do it faster yourself.
3. Report it on cybercrime.gov.in, or call 1930.

I'm not going to assess how likely a loss is, and I'm not going to tell you it's fine. Your bank can act on the account right now; I can't.
